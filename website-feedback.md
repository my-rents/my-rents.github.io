# FEEDBACK 1

I dug through your ecosystem a bit, including the public app listings for MyRent Owner and related positioning around rental management.
Your biggest opportunity is not “making the website prettier.”
It’s making the website instantly legible.
Right now, most property-management SaaS sites accidentally speak in “software dialect”:
dashboards
integrations
automation
analytics
management
But landlords and property managers are secretly asking:
“Will this save me time?”
“Will this make me more money?”
“Will this stop Airbnb chaos at 11:47 PM?”
“Can I trust this with my business?”
That means your site should feel less like a control panel brochure and more like a revenue machine wearing a tailored suit. 🏨⚡
What I would improve immediately

1. Your hero section needs sharper positioning
   Most SaaS homepages waste the first screen with generic phrases.
   Instead of:
   “Property management made easy”
   You want something closer to:
   Manage all your rentals from one place.
   Sync bookings, automate guest communication, track revenue, and stay in control without spreadsheets or chaos.
   Then immediately:
   CTA #1: “Book a demo”
   CTA #2: “Watch 2-minute tour”
   And visually:
   One crystal-clear dashboard screenshot
   One “before vs after” workflow graphic
   Maybe a small occupancy/revenue chart
   The first 5 seconds should answer:
   What is this?
   Who is it for?
   Why is it better?
   What should I click?
   Right now many property SaaS sites bury that clarity under feature soup 🍲
2. Stop selling features. Sell outcomes.
   This is the single largest conversion upgrade most SaaS companies miss.
   Instead of:
   Channel manager
   Smart pricing
   Analytics
   Guest messaging
   Translate them into business outcomes:
   Feature Outcome
   Channel sync “Avoid double bookings”
   Automated messaging “Save 10+ hours/week”
   Revenue dashboard “Spot underpriced nights instantly”
   Smart locks integration “Enable self check-in”
   Your copy should feel like:
   “Here’s the stress we remove.”
   Not:
   “Here’s our software architecture.”
3. Your homepage likely needs a better information hierarchy

   Section order
   Hero

   Core benefits
   Integrations
   Testimonials
   Pricing
   FAQ
   CTA footer

   You want narrative gravity.
   Every section should naturally pull the user downward.

   guest data

   operations

   “Trusted by X properties”
   Testimonials with real faces
   Review screenshots
   Integration logos
   Uptime/security section
   GDPR/privacy language
   Support response time
   “Based in [country]”
   Case studies
   Even tiny trust cues massively improve SaaS conversion.

# FEEDBACK 2

Since your product is a mobile app (with a web portal for tenants), your website’s primary goal is to drive app downloads and build trust. Currently, it seems like your website acts as a simple placeholder. If you want it to become an acquisition engine, here is a strategic roadmap to dramatically improve its Design, Marketing, and SEO.

1. Web Design & Marketing (CRO) Tips
   To get more people to download the app, your website needs to be conversion-optimized.
   Revamp the Hero Section (Above the Fold): Within 3 seconds, a visitor should know exactly what you do.
   Instead of: "How to start / Download the app."
   Try a strong Value Proposition: "Take Control of Your Rental Properties Anywhere."
   Subheadline: "The all-in-one app for landlords to track expenses, manage leases, and communicate with tenants effortlessly."
   Calls to Action (CTAs): Prominent "Download on the App Store" and "Get it on Google Play" badges.
   Show the App in Action (High-Quality Mockups): People need to visualize the software before downloading it. Include clean, modern phone mockups displaying your best features: the financial dashboard, the tenant portal link, and the property gallery.
   Focus on Benefits, Not Just Features: Translate technical features into marketing benefits.
   Feature: "Expense Tracking & Bill Management" ➡️ Benefit: "Maximize Your ROI: Track every expense and never miss a tax deduction."
   Feature: "Document Center" ➡️ Benefit: "Keep Tenants Happy: Give them a secure, private portal for leases and receipts."
   Leverage Social Proof: I see your app has over 5,000+ downloads on Google Play. Flaunt that! Add a banner saying "Trusted by over 5,000 landlords globally." Add 3-4 glowing user reviews with star ratings to build instant trust.
2. SEO (Search Engine Optimization) Tips
   Currently, a quick search for your domain (site:myrents-app.com) shows very little indexation on Google. Here is how to capture organic search traffic:
   Create Feature-Specific Pages: Right now, your site is likely a single landing page. Create individual pages for your core features (e.g., myrents-app.com/features/expense-tracking, .../tenant-portal, .../lease-management). This allows you to rank for specific Google searches like "best rental expense tracking app."
   Start a Content Hub / Blog for Landlords: The real estate management niche has massive search volume. Write high-quality articles targeting questions your users ask:
   "How to organize rental property receipts"
   "What landlords need to know about lease renewals"
   "Best apps for DIY landlords 2026"
   Multilingual SEO (Huge Opportunity): I noticed your app is localized in Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, and more. Your website should be too! Implement a multilingual site structure using hreflang tags (e.g., myrents-app.com/es/). Ranking for terms like "app para gestión de alquileres" or "gestione affitti pro" in local markets is much less competitive than in English.
   Technical SEO Basics: Ensure your site is connected to Google Search Console. Make sure your load speed is blazing fast (optimized images/mockups) and your meta titles/descriptions actually pitch the app (e.g., Title: "MyRents | The Ultimate App for Property Managers & Landlords").
3. Excellent Website Examples to Emulate
   To see these principles in action, check out how top players in the PropTech (Property Technology) space design their sites:
   Landlord Studio (landlordstudio.com): This is an excellent example because they also focus heavily on a mobile app experience. Notice their clean hero section, app store badges, phone mockups, and extensive blog for landlords.
   Stessa (stessa.com): Look at their site for inspiration on how to display financial reporting. They use clean graphics, emphasize "free," and clearly highlight the outcome (maximizing profits).
   TenantCloud (tenantcloud.com): Notice how they clearly segment their audience. They explain exactly what the software does step-by-step and emphasize the "Tenant Portal" feature very well.
   Hemlane (hemlane.com): A great example of using clean, friendly design and simple icons to break down complex property management tasks into digestible marketing copy.
   Next Steps: I would recommend starting with a wireframe for a new homepage. Put a strong headline at the top, add a phone mockup of your app's dashboard next to it, place the app store buttons right below the text, and add a "Features" breakdown as the user scrolls.
